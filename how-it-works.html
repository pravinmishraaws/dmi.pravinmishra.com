How DMI works

No self-reported progress. No participation trophies. Your real GitHub work is reviewed every day, graded automatically, and published. Here's the exact system.

The Weekly Loop

One week in DMI

The same loop, every week, for 14 weeks

Saturday — Live session day

Pravin opens with the helicopter view of the week's topic and teaches the key concepts. Then 3–4 assignments are taught live with theory and hands-on demos — followed by peer reviews and Champion of the Week.

Sunday — Self-study day

Re-watch the session, take notes, revise the concepts, and start your assignment in your own GitHub fork of the DMI template repo.

Monday–Thursday — Execution days

Build the assignments in your fork — real files, real commits, your repo. Live assignment help from co-mentors in the evenings, and doubts cleared in the Discord community.

Friday — Deadline day

Push your completed work to GitHub by end of day. Your work lives in public — that's the point, it becomes evidence.

Every day — Automated review

The review agent checks every file of your work against the week's checklist and updates your public badge page and the leaderboard — daily, not just at week's end.

Every week — Share what you built

A weekly LinkedIn post and blog post about what you built earn points too — and build your public presence.

Self-Paced students follow the same loop at their own pace — same assignments, same daily grading, no live sessions.

Scoring

How points are earned

Graded automatically, the same rules for everyone

WhatPoints
Each assignment file passing all checks20
Weekly LinkedIn post (URL in your progress table)10
Weekly blog post (must load + credit DMI)30
Live-session attendance (per slot: Morning · Mid · Evening)10 each

Live-session attendance applies to the live cohort and counts from Week 4 onward.

Real work only. Placeholder text scores 0. Copied or untouched files score 0. Answers under 50 real words score 0.
No pre-filling ahead. Grading is capped at the cohort's current week — you can't stack up points by pasting future weeks early.
Every run is kept. Score history is stored permanently, so any result can be looked up — not argued about.
The Result

A public profile that proves it

Every student gets a public badge page: week-by-week scores, passing checks, LinkedIn and blog links — updated automatically after every review run. This page is public. Put it in your CV. Hiring managers can click every claim.
