How grading works

Graded by a program, not by whoever had time

Every night a program clones every student’s repository, applies the same checks to everyone, and publishes the result to a page anyone can open. No mentor queue, no “it depends who marked you.”

✅

What it checks exactly

Provable things, with one right answer. Does the file exist at the path the course template expects? Is it still the untouched template — or a copy of the answer key? Does your blog post actually link back to your own badge page? Same input, same result, every time.

📏

What it approximates

A few checks stand in for judgement, and we say so rather than dressing them up. “At least 50 words of real writing” is counted after stripping code blocks, so a big paste can’t pass as prose. Any leftover placeholder text scores zero, with no partial credit.

🧑‍🏫

What it deliberately doesn’t

It does not decide whether your answer is correct, whether your reasoning is sound, or whether you understood it. That stays with your mentors in the live sessions. A passing score proves your work is original, complete and your own — not that it’s right.

It tells you the build passed. It doesn’t tell you the code is good.

That’s the honest boundary, and it’s why the score is worth something. Every mark is on permanent record, so “why did my score change?” is a lookup rather than an argument — and your feedback page lists exactly what to fix, in your own words back to you, the morning after you push.
