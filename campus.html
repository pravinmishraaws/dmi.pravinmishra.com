The Process

How an institutional partnership works

Three steps, and a written proposal before anything is committed to.

1

We scope it in writing

Tell us the department, the student group and your semester calendar. You get a written proposal for your institution — cohort size, dates, visibility mode, success metrics and fee — before any commitment.

2

Agreement, then nomination

We work to your institution's standard MoU or services-agreement template, or provide a draft. Your department nominates students; we make the final selection on merit, after a short assignment.

3

Delivery and reporting

Daily automated grading, progress reports through the cohort, a standing review with your nominated academic owner, and an end-of-cohort report you may cite in your own accreditation and placement reporting.

Student Records

Public or private — your decision

Chosen before the cohort starts, so it can be reviewed against your institution's own policies.

ModeWho can see a student's recordAvailability
Public Profiles, leaderboard and badge pages are openly visible, so a graduate can point an employer at verifiable evidence of what they built. This is the default, and the only mode on our open cohorts. Standard
Private Profiles, leaderboard, badges and the cohort's Discord channel are published at a private, unlisted address shared only with your institution and the students themselves — not indexed by search engines, and not linked from any public page. Licensed partnerships
To be precise about what Private means: access is controlled by the address rather than by a login. The link is unguessable and is never published or indexed, but anyone it is forwarded to can open it. If your institution requires access restricted to authenticated, named accounts, we are glad to scope that in the agreement rather than leave it assumed. Under Private, an individual student may also ask for their own profile to be made public — for example when applying for a role — and may reverse that at any time.
Where student data is held. The student's own work stays in the student's own repository; we read it to grade it, and ownership does not transfer to us. What we hold is the grading record — name, account handle, weekly scores, submission and completion status, and links to work the student published. It is held entirely within the European Union. The CloudAdvisory Oy is a Finnish company operating under the EU General Data Protection Regulation, and a student may withdraw publication consent in writing at any time.
Being Straight With You

What the certificate means — and what it doesn't

Two things worth stating plainly before your placement cell commits to anything.

📜 The certificate is earned, not given for attending

A student receives it only after completing every assignment and project, and only where the co-mentors judge that the student is capable of working independently on a real project in a real company. Participation alone does not earn it — which is precisely what makes it worth something in your placement reporting.

⚠️ There is no job guarantee

DMI is a training programme. It does not promise employment, placement, an internship offer or any career outcome, and we will not suggest otherwise. What it does produce is a complete, auditable record of what each student built — evidence your placement cell can put in front of an employer.

Accreditation & Reporting

An end-of-cohort report you can actually cite

Accreditation bodies are shifting toward demonstrable, verified competencies. That is exactly what a graded cohort produces.

🎓

India — NEP 2020 skill credits

The National Education Policy requires vocational and skill credits as part of every degree programme. DMI is a ready-made, externally delivered skill track with a per-student graded record behind it.

📊

India — NAAC Criterion V

Student Support & Progression is a weighted component of the institutional SSR, scored on progression and employability-skill development. A cohort generates real, per-student completion data you can cite directly.

✅

India — NBA graduate competencies

The revised SAR format calls for demonstrable, verified graduate competencies rather than completed coursework alone. Daily-graded, auditable output is built for that standard.

🇲🇾

Malaysia — MQA and SETARA

The same end-of-cohort report is issued for reporting to MQA, SETARA or equivalent, with the graded record behind every figure available for inspection.

🌍

Everywhere else

Reporting formats vary by country, institution and accreditation cycle. Tell us the template you have to file against and we will issue the report to match it, rather than leave your team to apportion our figures.

🔍

Every figure is checkable

Nothing in the report is a number only we can see. Each student's score traces back to a public graded record, assignment by assignment, with the date it was scored.

Commercial Framework

Licensed by institution, not per seat

Priced in a written proposal for your institution, not from a page.

Institutions are licensed by institution and by semester — so the term matches your academic calendar — in capacity bands, from a single pilot cohort up to a full institutional licence. Automated grading is what makes that possible: the marginal cost of an additional student is low, and the saving is passed on rather than kept. Mentor support scales with the licence — larger cohorts are supported by more co-mentors, not by less attention per student.

Fees are regional, and quoted per institution. We do not publish a single global rate card, because what is right for a college in Tamil Nadu is not what is right for a university in the Gulf. Tell us your institution and the cohort you have in mind and you will get a written proposal with the fee, the scope and the next band stated in it — so the step from a first cohort to a wider programme is a known figure rather than a fresh negotiation.

We are glad to work to your institution's standard MoU or services-agreement template, or to provide a draft for your office to review — whichever is faster for your internal process. We would normally propose scoping a first agreement to one cohort with a defined term, rather than an open-ended framework.
